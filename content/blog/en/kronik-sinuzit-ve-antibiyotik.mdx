One of the sentences I hear most often in clinic is: "I take antibiotics every month and it never clears." That sentence usually means the wrong illness is being treated.

## Acute and chronic sinusitis are not the same disease

**Acute sinusitis** usually starts after an upper respiratory infection: facial pain, blockage, coloured discharge. The important part is that most acute sinusitis is viral and resolves on its own within 7–10 days, without antibiotics.

**Chronic sinusitis** lasts longer than 12 weeks. Here the problem is not bacteria but a lining that stays permanently inflamed. That inflammation blocks the sinus openings; a blocked sinus cannot ventilate; a sinus that cannot ventilate becomes inflamed again. An antibiotic touches one point of that cycle, and only temporarily.

## When an antibiotic is genuinely warranted

The situations are limited:

- Symptoms lasting more than 10 days with no improvement at all
- An illness that improved and then clearly worsened again after day five or six
- High fever, severe one-sided facial pain, or visible swelling

Outside those, repeated antibiotic courses achieve two things: resistance develops, and the real problem is delayed.

## What treatment looks like in chronic sinusitis

The core of treatment is reducing inflammation:

- **Topical nasal corticosteroid.** It has to be used with the right technique, regularly, and for a long time. A few days and then stopping does nothing.
- **Saline rinsing.** It looks trivial and it is not. It clears the lining and helps the spray reach where it needs to.
- **Treating allergy, if present.** Sinusitis cannot be controlled while allergic rhinitis is left untreated.

## Where surgery comes in

Surgery is considered when symptoms persist despite medical treatment being applied properly. The aim of [endoscopic sinus surgery](/en/tedaviler/endoskopik-sinus-cerrahisi) is not to "clean out" the sinus but to open the blocked outflow tracts so the sinus can clear itself again.

Spray and rinsing continue after surgery. The operation does not replace medical treatment — it creates the conditions for it to work.

> A CT scan alone is not enough to make the diagnosis. People without symptoms can show sinus changes on a scan. The decision is made together with the examination and the symptoms.
