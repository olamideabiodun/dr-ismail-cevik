Nasal polyps are benign, pendulous swellings that grow out of a long-inflamed nasal and sinus lining. They do not hurt. That is why patients usually notice their consequences rather than the polyps themselves.

## The three commonest complaints

1. **Blockage that never clears.** A spray opens it briefly, then it closes again.
2. **Loss of smell and taste.** Patients typically describe it as "food lost its flavour". Loss of smell is often the first sign.
3. **Post-nasal drip and a feeling of pressure in the head.**

## Why do polyps form?

A polyp is a consequence, not a cause. Underneath it is chronic inflammation of the lining, and behind that inflammation you often find:

- Asthma
- Allergic rhinitis
- Aspirin sensitivity, particularly alongside asthma

So treatment does not end with removing the polyps. If the underlying inflammation is not treated, they return. I explain it to patients like this: removing polyps is like mowing grass. If you do not change the soil, the grass grows back.

## How treatment is planned

**Medication first.** Topical corticosteroid sprays are the centre of treatment. Widespread polyps may need a short course of oral steroid. Saline rinsing should not be skipped, because it helps the spray reach the lining.

**Surgery if the response is insufficient.** [Endoscopic surgery](/en/tedaviler/endoskopik-sinus-cerrahisi) clears the polyps and opens blocked sinus outflow tracts. The purpose is to open up areas the spray could not reach.

**Treatment after surgery.** This is the most critical stage, and the most commonly abandoned one. Where spray and rinsing are not continued afterwards, the recurrence rate rises markedly.

## When not to wait

Do not delay assessment if you have:

- One-sided nasal blockage
- Recurrent nosebleeds
- Loss of smell on one side only
- Swelling around the eye, or double vision

One-sided findings can indicate something other than polyps and need detailed investigation.
