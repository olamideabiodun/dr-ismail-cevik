The question I am asked most after rhinoplasty is: "When will my nose settle?" The honest answer is longer than most patients expect. Knowing the timeline in advance removes a great deal of unnecessary anxiety.

## The first week

The splint, and packing if it was used, come out during this period. The face is swollen and there may be bruising under the eyes. **No conclusion about the shape of the nose can be drawn this week.** What you are looking at is swelling, not your nose.

## Month one

The obvious swelling has largely gone. From the outside the nose starts to look "normal", and most people around you will not realise you had surgery. The tip, however, still sits thicker and higher than it eventually will. That is expected.

## Month three

This is the turning point. Roughly **70–80%** of the swelling has resolved. The bridge has definition and the profile has largely settled.

Two things come up often at this stage:

- **The tip still feels slightly thick.** The tip is where swelling resolves last, and in patients with thicker skin it takes longer still.
- **Small asymmetries become noticeable.** Swelling does not resolve symmetrically; one side can run a few weeks ahead of the other. Small differences seen at three months are usually not permanent.

## Month six

The tip has definition and movement on smiling looks natural again. Most patients settle into the result at this point.

## Twelve months and beyond

The last fine changes complete during this period, and in thick skin it can extend to 18 months. **This is the earliest point at which a revision decision should be considered** — deciding sooner risks operating on a nose that has not finished settling.

## What you can do to help

- Come to your follow-up appointments; taping can help reduce swelling.
- Protect the nose from sun in the early months.
- Do not smoke — it noticeably delays tissue healing.
- Plan your return to heavy exercise and contact sport with your surgeon.

> The nose you see at three months is a draft of the nose you will see in a year. Do not draw final conclusions from a draft.
