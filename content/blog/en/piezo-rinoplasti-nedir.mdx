Shaping the bony vault is one of the decisive stages of rhinoplasty. Conventionally it is done with a chisel and a rasp. A piezo device instead cuts bone with ultrasonic vibration.

## The key difference: selectivity

The important property of a piezo tip is that it is **selective**. Ultrasonic vibration cuts hard tissue like bone but does not act on soft tissue — vessels, nerves, mucosa. If the tip touches mucosa by accident, it does not cut it.

In practice that has two consequences:

- Bone can be worked **millimetre by millimetre, under control**.
- Trauma to the surrounding soft tissue stays more limited.

## Is it true that "there is no bruising"?

No. What is true is that because soft-tissue trauma is more limited, **bruising and swelling are usually less**. Saying there will be none is not realistic.

How much bruising occurs does not depend on the instrument alone. Skin thickness, bleeding tendency, whatever else is done in the same session, and how well the patient follows post-operative instructions all matter.

## Where I favour it

- A prominent bony hump on the bridge
- A wide or asymmetric bony vault
- [Revision](/en/tedaviler/revizyon-rinoplasti) patients whose bone has been left irregular by earlier surgery

## What it does not change

Piezo does not change the **plan**. How your nose will look comes from the measurements, from setting the right goals and from how tip support is rebuilt — not from the device.

Nor does it shorten recovery. Most of the swelling still resolves at around three months, with subtle changes continuing for a year. I wrote about that in detail in [The third month after rhinoplasty](/en/blog/rinoplasti-3-ay).

> Technique is not the only determinant of the result. The same device produces different outcomes under different plans.
